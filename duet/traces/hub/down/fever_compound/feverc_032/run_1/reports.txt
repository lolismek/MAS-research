===== edge 1 =====
I investigated the claim that Pocahontas became a celebrity and was noted in the context and culture she lived in, specifically focusing on her 1616–1617 visit to England.

**Findings:**
Historical evidence strongly supports the claim. During her time in England, Pocahontas (known as Rebecca Rolfe) was treated as a celebrity and a major public curiosity. She was presented to King James I and Queen Anne, attended high-society events such as masques at Whitehall Palace, and was the subject of an engraving by Simon van de Passe that was circulated to promote the Virginia Company. Contemporary accounts describe her as causing a "sensation" among the English upper crust. Furthermore, she was noted in her native context as the daughter of the paramount chief Powhatan and in the English context as a symbol of the "civilized savage" and the success of the mission to convert Native Americans.

**Answer:**
SUPPORTS

===== edge 2 =====
I investigated the historical records and contemporary accounts regarding Pocahontas's status in both her native Powhatan culture and the English colonial society she interacted with.

**Findings:**
The evidence strongly indicates that Pocahontas was a prominent and noted figure in both cultures.

*   **English Colonial Society:** She achieved a level of celebrity status, particularly during her 1616 trip to London. Contemporary accounts describe her as being "elegantly fêted" and presented to the English court, including an audience with King James I. She was used as a promotional figure for the Jamestown colony, described as an example of the "civilized savage."
*   **Native Powhatan Culture:** She was noted as the "favorite daughter" of the powerful paramount chief Wahunsenaca (Powhatan). Accounts describe her as her father's "delight and darling." She held a specific diplomatic role as a peacemaker, frequently delivering food to the starving colonists and negotiating for the release of Powhatan prisoners.

**Answer Reached:**
SUPPORTS

**Key Evidence:**
*   **London Fame:** Her 1616 visit to London where she was fêted and met the King.
*   **Diplomatic Role:** Her active participation in peace negotiations and food deliveries (noted by John Smith and William Strachey).
*   **Family Status:** Her status as the daughter of the paramount chief, making her a key figure in Powhatan politics and English perception.
