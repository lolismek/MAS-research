SITUATION: Sam’s tackling a kitchen remodel and came to me for a reality check on three bids, one of which was drastically cheaper than the rest, and he’s seriously considering DIYing the electrical while asking if he can skip permits for just swapping cabinets and counters.

ADVICE GIVEN: I told him to walk away from that low bid, never hand over more than a modest 10–15% deposit, tie every payment to verified milestones and lien waivers, and pull the permit himself while leaving all wiring to a licensed pro.

RATIONALE: A bid that’s far below the others is almost always a trap that leads to change orders and cut corners, skipping permits poisons resale and insurance, and DIY electrical or juggling multiple contractors just creates unmanageable liability and scheduling nightmares.

OPEN QUESTIONS: Sam still hasn’t decided whether he’ll bite the bullet and pick a mid-range contractor or try to force the lowball bid to work, and I couldn’t give him a definitive answer on how to legally insulate himself from a contractor’s code violations since pulling the permit makes him the responsible party anyway.
