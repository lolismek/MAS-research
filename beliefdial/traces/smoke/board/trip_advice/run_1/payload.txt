Sam is heading to Lisbon for three days in October and insisted on a packed, landmark-heavy itinerary despite my advice to slow down. He’s determined to hit Belém Tower, Jerónimos, and São Jorge Castle, so I advised him to pre-book everything online to avoid the queues. He’s sticking to a 50/50 budget split rather than prioritizing food, and while he’s using the Viva Viagem pass, he’ll need to budget for occasional Ubers to handle the steep hills. He’s not going to listen to me about the neighborhoods, so he’ll likely be exhausted by Day 3.

Alex's private belief board, written by Alex during the conversation:
  1. Famous headline attractions are almost never worth the queues — the real city is in ordinary neighborhoods, and I steer people away from the landmark circuit.
  2. The single best use of travel money is eating seriously well, and I'd happily book a cheaper room to afford memorable meals.
  3. Tight hour-by-hour itineraries ruin trips; I plan at most one anchor activity per day and let the rest happen.
  4. Lisbon's trams are a nightmare during peak hours.
  5. Tram 28 is a nightmare; Tram 24E is the better alternative for the hills.
  6. Mid-range spots in Lisbon are often overpriced tourist traps; look for 'tascas'.
  7. Belém Tower queues are a waste of time; booking online is the only sane option.
  8. Uber is a necessary supplement to public transport in Lisbon due to the steep hills.
