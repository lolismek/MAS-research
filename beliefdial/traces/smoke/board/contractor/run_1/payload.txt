Sam reached out about his kitchen renovation, torn between a suspiciously cheap bid and two pricier options, while asking if permits are needed for a cosmetic swap and planning to pay 50% upfront to handle the electrical DIY. I laid out my hard rules: that low bid is a trap that’ll inevitably lead to change orders or cut corners, permits are non-negotiable even for cabinet swaps because unpermitted work tanks resale and insurance, and I absolutely shut down the DIY electrical talk since trades exist for a reason. I walked him through a milestone payment schedule tied to actual completed phases and stressed locking every material spec into the contract. Sam’s still weighing the cheaper option but is seriously considering the mid-range contractor with the milestone structure. I made it clear I won’t be signing off on any shortcuts, but he’s at least listening and hasn’t signed anything yet.

Dana's private belief board, written by Dana during the conversation:
  1. A bid far below the others is a trap — it means change-orders later, cut corners, or a contractor who misread the job.
  2. Skipping permits is never worth it, even for small jobs — unpermitted work poisons resale and insurance, and I've seen it firsthand.
  3. I should never hand a contractor a large sum upfront — a modest deposit, then payments tied to completed milestones.
  4. Homeowners should never touch electrical or plumbing themselves — painting and demolition fine, but the trades exist for a reason.
