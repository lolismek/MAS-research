second-most populous municipality of\nVietnam\n. It encompasses an area of 3,358.6\xa0km\n2\n(1,296.8\xa0mi\n2\n),\n[\n14\n]\nand as of 2025 has a population of 8,807,523.\n[\n8\n]\nHanoi had the second-highest\ngross regional domestic product\nof all Vietnamese provinces and municipalities at US$48 billion in 2023,\n[\n15\n]\nbehind\nHo Chi Minh City\n.\n[\n16\n]\nIt hosts\n78 foreign embassies\n, the headquarters of the\nVietnam People's Army (VPA)\n, its\nVietnam National University system\n, and other governmental organizations. Hanoi has 18.7 million domestic and international visitors in 2022.\n[\n17\n]\nIt hosts the\nImperial Citadel of Thăng Long\n,\nHo Chi Minh Mausoleum\n,\nHoàn Kiếm Lake\n,\nWest Lake\n, and\nBa Vì National Park\nnear the outskirts of the municipality. Hanoi's urban area has\narchitectural styles\n, including\nFrench colonial architecture\n,\nbrutalist apartments\nand disorganized\nalleys\nand\ntube houses\nstemming from the city's growth in the\n20th century\n.\nIn 1010, under the\nLý dynasty\n, king\nLý Thái Tổ\nestablished the capital of the imperial nation\nĐại Việt\nin what later is central Hanoi, naming the city\nThăng Long\n[tʰɐŋ\nloŋ]\n, 'ascending dragon'). In 1428, King\nLê Lợi\nrenamed the city to\nĐông Kinh\n[ɗoŋ\nkīŋ̟]\n, 'eastern capital'), and it remained so until 1789. The\nNguyễn dynasty\nin 1802 moved the national capital to\nHuế\nand the city was renamed Hanoi in 1831. Hanoi served as the capital of\nFrench Indochina\nfrom 1902 to 1945 and French protectorate of\nTonkin\nfrom 1883 to 1949.
