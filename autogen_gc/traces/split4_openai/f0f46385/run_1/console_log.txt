It is the most populous city in the country, covering an area of 243\xa0km\n2\n(94\xa0sq\xa0mi) with a population of 2,075,600 as of 2024\n[update]\n.\n[\n9\n]\nGreater Kuala Lumpur\n, which itself includes the\nKlang Valley\n, is an\nurban agglomeration\nof 8.81 million people as of 2024. It is also home to the\nMalaysian parliament\n(consisting of the\nDewan Rakyat\nand the\nDewan Negara\n) and the\nIstana Negara\n, the official residence of the\nmonarch\n(\nYang di-Pertuan Agong\n). Kuala Lumpur was first developed around 1857 as a town serving the\ntin mines\nof the region, and important figures such as\nYap Ah Loy\nand\nFrank Swettenham\nwere instrumental in the early development of the city during the late 19th century. It served as the capital of\nSelangor\nfrom 1880 until 1978. Kuala Lumpur was the founding capital of the\nFederation of Malaya\nand its successor, Malaysia. The city remained the seat of the executive and judicial branches of the\nMalaysian federal government\nuntil these were relocated to\nPutrajaya\nin early 1999.\n[\n10\n]\nHowever, some sections of the political bodies still remain in Kuala Lumpur. Kuala Lumpur is well connected with neighbouring urban metro regions such as\nPetaling Jaya\nvia the rapidly expanding\nKlang Valley Integrated Transit System\n. Naypyidaw is notable for its unusual combination of large size and very low population density.\n[\n6\n]\n[\n7\n]\nThe city hosted the 24th and 25th\nASEAN Summit\n, the 3rd\nBIMSTEC\nSummit, the\nNinth East Asia Summit\n, the\n2013 Southeast Asian Games\nand the\n2014 AFC U-19 Championship\n.\nEtymology\n[\nedit\n]\nNaypyidaw\nis\nBurmese\nfor "abode of the king", and is generally translated as "royal capital",\n[\n8\n]\n"seat of the king",\n[\n9\n]\nor "abode of kings".\n[\n3\n]\nTraditionally, it was used as a suffix to the names of royal capitals, such as\nMandalay\n, which was called Yadanabon Naypyidaw (\nရတနာပုံနေပြည်တော်\n).\nHistory\n[\nedit\n]\nNaypyidaw was founded on a\ngreenfield site\nnear\nPyinmana\n, about 320 kilometres (200\xa0mi) north of the old capital,\nYangon\n. Construction started in 2002 and was completed by 2012.\n[\n10\n]\nMilitary construction projects were overseen by the Quartermaster General\nTin Aung Myint Oo\n, while civilian construction projects were overseen by\nThein Nyunt\n, an army colonel.\n[\n11\n]\nConstruction of the city was carried out by three main groups, including the military\'s engineering corps, contractors with familial ties to high-ranking military officials, and the military\'s conglomerate,\nMyanma Economic Holdings Limited\n.\n[\n11\n]\nConstruction was plagued with corruption and nepotism.\n[\n11\n]\nIt is estimated that the planned city development reached US$4 billion.\n[\n12\n]\n[\n9\n]\nOn 27 March 2006, more than 12,000 troops marched in the new capital in its first public event: a massive military parade to mark\nArmed Forces Day\n, the anniversary of Burma\'s 1945 revolution against the\nJapanese occupation\n. Filming was restricted to the concrete parade ground, which contains enormous sculptures of Kings\nAnawrahta\n,\nBayinnaung\n, and\nAlaungpaya\n. The city occupies 1,568.7 square kilometres (605.7\xa0sq\xa0mi) in the\nChao Phraya River\ndelta in\ncentral Thailand\nand has an estimated population of 11.4 million people as of 2024,\n[\ncitation needed\n]\n15.9% of the country\'s population. Bangkok was at the heart of the modernization of Siam during the late 19th century, as the country faced pressures from the West. The city was at the centre of the country\'s political struggles throughout the 20th century, as Siam—later renamed Thailand—abolished absolute monarchy, adopted constitutional rule, and underwent numerous coups and several uprisings. The city is now a regional force in finance, business, and pop culture. It is an international hub for transport and health care, and has emerged as a centre for the arts, fashion, and entertainment. The city is known for its street life and cultural landmarks, as well as its\nred-light districts\n.
